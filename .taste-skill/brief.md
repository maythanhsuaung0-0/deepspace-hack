# HackAtlas design brief

## Subject inventory
- The name joins the urgency of a hackathon with the permanence of an atlas.
- A hackathon begins with a date, deadline, place, format, and source.
- Builders compare several cities and time zones at once.
- The same event must make sense as a map point and as a list entry.
- Online, hybrid, and in-person are the three physical modes.
- Topics behave like a map legend: AI, climate, civic tech, hardware, open source, and games.
- Coordinates are useful evidence, not decoration.
- Demo records must announce that they are synthetic.
- The primary ritual is narrowing the world, selecting one point, and opening its source.
- Printed atlases use paper, limited ink, legends, plates, folios, and folded panels.

## Argument
- Hackathons are scattered; HackAtlas gives them one legible field — proved by the combined map and catalog.
- A useful atlas narrows quickly — proved by search, format, and topic filters over one shared dataset.
- Place and detail should stay connected — proved by shared selection between map markers and list entries.
- Discovery should end at the source — proved by a clearly labeled source action on every selected event.
- The first catalog is honest — proved by visible demo labeling and synthetic source notes.

## Direction
